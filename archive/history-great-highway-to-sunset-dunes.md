This note tells, with dates and vote counts, how the Upper Great Highway became Sunset Dunes park, and what followed: three ballot measures, a recall, a new supervisor. It also covers the L Taraval rebuild, the housing at 2550 Irving, the Family Zoning Plan and Sunset Boulevard. It gives each side's argument without choosing one. Status is as of November 10, 2026.

## The Upper Great Highway closes to cars in April 2020, then the weekend compromise of 2021 and 2022

The Upper Great Highway, the two miles between Lincoln Way and Sloat Boulevard, first closed to cars in April 2020, under the pandemic stay-at-home order, so people had room to walk and bike apart. Before that it carried about 140,000 vehicles a week and was shut about 27 times a year for sand removal and flooding. In August 2021 Supervisor Gordon Mar and Mayor London Breed struck a compromise: cars on weekdays, people on weekends and holidays, with the road closing at noon each Friday and reopening at 6 a.m. Monday. In December 2022 the Board of Supervisors voted 9 to 2 to keep that schedule as a pilot through December 31, 2025. Supervisors Myrna Melgar and Connie Chan voted no; their amendment to start the closure at 6 a.m. Saturday instead failed 8 to 3. Mar argued the split balanced a promenade with car access. Chan and Melgar said the Friday start made trips to the VA hospital, schools and park soccer fields harder.

Sources: [Richmond Review/Sunset Beacon, Board votes to keep Upper Great Highway closed on weekends (January 2023)](https://richmondsunsetnews.com/2023/01/06/board-votes-to-keep-ugh-closed-to-cars-on-weekends-for-3-more-years/), [Wikipedia, Great Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Highway).

## Proposition I, November 2022: voters reject bringing cars back full time

Proposition I was the first citywide vote on the Great Highway. It would have put cars back on the Upper Great Highway seven days a week, as before the pandemic. In November 2022 it lost, 65.11 percent no to 34.89 percent yes. Supporters of Prop I argued that west side residents needed the road for north-south trips and that the closure pushed traffic onto neighborhood streets. Opponents wanted to keep the weekend promenade; the planning group SPUR also said the measure would have blocked the city's plan to protect sewer pipes from erosion south of Sloat. The citywide number hid a local split. The SF Standard later reported that precincts with more Asian American voters were more supportive of reopening the road, while majority-white precincts tended to oppose Prop I. The defeat let the Board of Supervisors write the weekend schedule into law a month later.

## Proposition K, November 2024: the city votes yes, the Sunset votes no

Proposition K asked voters to close the Upper Great Highway to private cars every day and make it a park. Supervisors Joel Engardio and Myrna Melgar put it on the ballot in June 2024 with Mayor Breed's support. In November 2024 it passed citywide, 54.73 percent to 45.27 percent. The west side voted the other way: about 64 percent of District 4 voters said no, and most residents living closest to the road voted against it. Supporters argued the city could have an oceanfront park, and that erosion was already forcing the road south of Sloat to close. Opponents, including merchant Albert Chow and the Chinese American Democratic Club, argued that tens of thousands of cars a day would shift onto nearby streets and that the measure cut against drivers, seniors, small businesses and working families. The road closed for good on March 14, 2025. A lawsuit to overturn Prop K was rejected by a judge in early January 2026.

## Sunset Dunes park gets its name and opens, April 2025

Sunset Dunes is the park on the former Upper Great Highway, from Lincoln Way to Sloat Boulevard: two miles long and, by Rec and Park's count, 50 acres. The name came from the public. Rec and Park took about 4,200 suggestions between March 1 and 16, 2025, narrowed them to five finalists (Sunset Dunes, Playland Parkway, Great Parkway, Fog Line and Plover Parkway), ran a citywide survey, and the Recreation and Park Commission made it official on April 9, 2025. The park opened Saturday, April 12, 2025, with a ribbon cutting at 2:13 p.m. About 13,000 people came that day, and Mission Local found lines out the door at shops on Noriega. It was not all celebration. A caravan of cars with yellow "Recall Joel Engardio" signs circled the Lower Great Highway honking, and a 60-foot mural by Emily Fromm had been vandalized in March and repainted by volunteers. Rec and Park calls it the largest pedestrianization project in California history.

Sources: [SF Recreation and Parks, Sunset Dunes](https://sfrecpark.org/GreatHighwayPilot), [Mission Local, Sunset Dunes opens (April 2025)](https://missionlocal.org/2025/04/sunset-dunes-great-highway-park-opens/), [Wikipedia, Sunset Dunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunset_Dunes), [Streetsblog SF, Sunset Dunes one year out](https://sf.streetsblog.org/2026/04/09/sunset-dunes-one-year-out-they-built-it-and-people-came).

## What is in Sunset Dunes park: art, play areas, events and planting

Sunset Dunes has a skate space, a bike skills course and pump track, outdoor fitness equipment, a nature play area, hammocks, log seats cut from storm-felled Golden Gate Park trees, two water fountains and a temporary restroom at Noriega. Shipwreck Cove, a 6,000 square foot pirate-themed playground at Kirkham Street, opened September 26, 2026. It cost $800,000, all from private donors, and has a one-year pilot permit. The art opened with 11 murals and 5 sculptures by 18 artists, all temporary and privately funded. In 2,961 public comments, 88.9 percent were positive. Critics mostly wanted the place left natural, or objected to paint on asphalt. In April 2026 the art's sponsors asked to keep four sculptures through April 2027: Rock Spinner, Wave Heart, Ocean Calling and Octavius the octopus. First-year programming included more than 75 events, among them the Great Hauntway at Halloween (about 10,000 trick-or-treaters), tai chi and a Cantonese dance class for seniors. Volunteers planted over 2,400 native dune grasses.

Sources: [SF Recreation and Parks, Sunset Dunes](https://sfrecpark.org/GreatHighwayPilot), [Sunset Dunes Temporary Public Art Update (April 2026)](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/Sunset_Dunes_Art_Update_2026.pdf), [Streetsblog SF, Sunset Dunes gets a pirate-themed playground](https://sf.streetsblog.org/2026/09/28/sunset-dunes-gets-a-pirate-themed-playground-arrr), [Streetsblog SF, Sunset Dunes one year out](https://sf.streetsblog.org/2026/04/09/sunset-dunes-one-year-out-they-built-it-and-people-came).

## Traffic studies after the Great Highway closed: what the counts show

The main traffic study is SFMTA's Outer Sunset report of July 25, 2025, comparing counts before and after the March 2025 closure. It found about 700 more vehicles a day on the Lower Great Highway, roughly one extra car a minute at rush hour, and about 2,000 more a day on Chain of Lakes Drive in Golden Gate Park. Sunset Boulevard carried 90 percent of its pre-pandemic volume, and Lincoln Way east of Chain of Lakes was 7 percent below. The 28 19th Avenue bus lost 1 to 2 minutes southbound at peak hours; the 29 Sunset averaged 30 seconds slower; the 18 46th Avenue did not change. The city added signals at Sloat and Skyline and at 41st Avenue and Lincoln, and planned more on Lincoln at La Playa and 45th. Later reviews found rush-hour speeds changed by about 1 to 1.5 miles per hour on most streets. Residents who oppose the closure describe it differently; one told KQED of bumper-to-bumper traffic toward 19th Avenue.

Sources: [SFMTA, New data on Great Highway road closure (July 2025)](https://www.sfmta.com/blog/new-data-great-highway-road-closure-how-were-supporting-your-trips), [Streetsblog SF, Sunset Dunes one year out](https://sf.streetsblog.org/2026/04/09/sunset-dunes-one-year-out-they-built-it-and-people-came), [KQED, One year of San Francisco's controversial beachside park](https://www.kqed.org/news/12079238/one-year-of-san-franciscos-controversial-beachside-park).

## The recall of Supervisor Joel Engardio, September 16, 2025

District 4 voters recalled Supervisor Joel Engardio on September 16, 2025, the first recall of a San Francisco supervisor in the city's history. The measure was called Proposition A. Election night results showed 64.6 percent in favor of removing him; two days later, with more ballots counted, it was 62.9 percent. Engardio conceded that night. The recall grew out of Proposition K. Organizers, who turned in 10,523 valid signatures in May 2025, argued that he had backed a citywide measure his own district opposed, and that it would worsen neighborhood traffic. Merchant Albert Chow's phrase was that no decisions should be made about the Sunset without the Sunset. Engardio argued that the park was a success, good for the coast, for business and for residents. His side raised more than $824,000, about three times the recall campaign's $250,000 or so. Mayor Daniel Lurie did not publicly back him.

Sources: [SF Standard, Engardio recall election results](https://sfstandard.com/2025/09/16/san-francisco-joel-engardio-recall-election-results/), [SF Standard, Night market canceled (July 2025)](https://sfstandard.com/2025/07/10/sunset-irving-street-night-market-cancelled-recall/).

## After the recall: Beya Alcaraz, Alan Wong, and the 2026 District 4 elections

After the recall, Mayor Daniel Lurie's first choice for District 4 supervisor, Isabella "Beya" Alcaraz, resigned after eight days in November 2025, amid questions about her Sunset pet store and her inexperience. Lurie then chose Alan Wong, a City College trustee who grew up in the Sunset and once worked for Supervisor Gordon Mar. Wong was sworn in December 1, 2025. He had voted no on Prop K. In January 2026 he tried to put a weekday reopening of the road on the June ballot, but only two other supervisors, Connie Chan and Chyanne Chen, signed on, one short. On June 2, 2026 Wong won the special election for the rest of the term. Wikipedia's tally has him at 46 percent of first choices, ahead of Natalie Gee (24), Albert Chow (17), Jeremy Greco (8) and David Lee (5), and 64 percent to Gee's 36 in the final round; election-night reports said 72 to 28. On November 3, 2026 he won a full four-year term.

Sources: [SF Standard, Alan Wong appointment](https://sfstandard.com/2025/11/30/sunset-supervisor-alan-wong-appointment/), [Sunset Beacon, Wong decisive victor in D4 race](https://richmondsunsetnews.com/2026/06/02/wong-decisive-victor-in-d-4-supe-race/), [Wikipedia, Alan Wong (politician)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Wong_(politician)), [Sunset Beacon, Sunset Dunes to remain open as Prop. ## Proposition G, November 3, 2026: voters keep Sunset Dunes, and where things stand now

Proposition G, the "Great Highway for Everyone Act," would have reopened the Upper Great Highway to cars on weekdays and kept Sunset Dunes as a park only on weekends and holidays. It reached the ballot by petition with 15,924 signatures (10,615 needed). It failed on November 3, 2026. The count as of November 9 was 213,671 no (65.6 percent) to 111,843 yes (34.3 percent); not yet certified. Supporters, led by Otto Pippenger and backed by Supervisor Alan Wong, called it a compromise and argued the closure sends drivers onto quiet streets and shuts out people who need cars. Opponents, led by Friends of Sunset Dunes, argued the park is well used and the question was settled. They raised over $821,000 to the yes side's roughly $160,000. The SF Standard found the eight precincts beside the park flipped, from 40 percent pro-park in 2024 to a majority in 2026. No new reopening effort has been announced. Wong says he will turn to traffic and safety on nearby streets.

## The Great Highway Extension south of Sloat and the Ocean Beach Climate Change Adaptation Project

South of Sloat Boulevard, the Great Highway Extension runs along an eroding bluff past the zoo to Skyline Boulevard. It is closing for a different reason than the road to the north: the ocean is taking it. The Board of Supervisors approved permanent closure in May 2024. Southbound lanes closed in March 2025; the SFMTA says access to the zoo by the extension stays until main construction. The SFPUC's Ocean Beach Climate Change Adaptation Project will then build a buried wall to protect the sewage treatment plant, pump station and recycled water plant, and a trail of more than a mile where the road is now. A 60-space parking lot is planned near Skyline; the 35-space lot at Sloat goes away. Major construction has slipped to late 2029 and should last about four years, with south Ocean Beach closed about six months of each. Smaller changes at Sloat and the zoo entrance start in early 2027. An open house is set for November 16, 2026 at the zoo.

Sources: [SFPUC, Ocean Beach Climate Change Adaptation Project](https://www.sfpuc.gov/en//construction-contracts/construction-projects/oceanbeach), [SFMTA, Zoo Access, Safety and Connectivity](https://www.sfmta.com/vi/node/45992), [SFMTA, New data on Great Highway road closure](https://www.sfmta.com/blog/new-data-great-highway-road-closure-how-were-supporting-your-trips), [SPUR, California's largest pedestrian project](https://www.spur.org/news/2024-12-09/californias-largest-pedestrian-project-expands-vision-spur-led-climate-adaptation).

## The L Taraval rebuild, 2019 to 2024

The L Taraval Improvement Project rebuilt Taraval Street from the zoo to West Portal, and it kept trains off the street for years. Planning and outreach ran from 2014 to 2017. Segment A, from the zoo to Sunset Boulevard, was built from 2019 to 2021. Segment B, from Sunset Boulevard to West Portal, followed from 2022 to 2024. L trains came back on September 28, 2024. The reason was safety. Before the project, SFMTA says, nearly ten people a year were hit by vehicles on Taraval, about half of them while stepping on or off a train into traffic. The $90 million job added concrete boarding islands, replaced track and overhead wires, replaced water and sewer lines (some more than a century old), added signals that give trains priority, and repaved the street, with new trees and crosswalk art. Some merchants say the cost was high: Albert Chow told Mission Local in 2026 that lost parking closed some Taraval businesses.

Sources: [SFMTA, L Taraval Improvement Project](https://www.sfmta.com/projects/l-taraval-improvement-project), [Mission Local, Sunset business recovery (October 2026)](https://missionlocal.org/?p=874791).

## 2550 Irving Street: the affordable housing fight and the finished building

2550 Irving Street is a 90-unit, all-affordable apartment building in the mid-Sunset, a block from Golden Gate Park, built by the nonprofit Tenderloin Neighborhood Development Corporation and designed by Pyatok Architects. It stands 78 feet tall. Fifteen homes are for formerly homeless veterans and 22 for families leaving homelessness; rents are set for households earning 30 to 80 percent of area median income. By July 2026 every unit was occupied. Getting there took a fight. The plan was unveiled in early 2021, and neighbors formed the Mid-Sunset Neighborhood Association to oppose it. About 200 opponents filled a church meeting in November 2021, with signs about toxic waste and about sunlight, and chants to recall Supervisor Gordon Mar. The association sued. Supporters pointed out that District 4 had gained only 26 net new affordable homes from 2010 to 2020. Because of a state law, SB 35, the project needed no Planning Commission vote. The plan was cut by about 50 units along the way.

Sources: [SF YIMBY, New residents finish moving into 2550 Irving Street (July 2026)](https://sfyimby.com/2026/07/new-residents-finish-moving-into-affordable-housing-at-2550-irving-street-in-san-francisco.html), [Cornell Real Estate Review, The battle to build affordable housing in western San Francisco (May 2022)](https://blog.realestate.cornell.edu/2022/05/30/the-battle-to-build-affordable-housing-units-in-western-san-francisco-an-analysis-of-the-proposed-2550-irving-street-project).

## The Family Zoning Plan, 2025 to 2026, and what it changes on Sunset streets

The Family Zoning Plan is Mayor Daniel Lurie's rezoning of the west and north sides of the city, required by the state so San Francisco has room for about 36,000 more homes. The Board of Supervisors approved it 7 to 4 on December 2, 2025, with new Supervisor Alan Wong voting yes, and it took effect January 12, 2026. In the Sunset, SFist reported, the height limit on Judah, Noriega and Taraval rose from 65 to 85 feet. Off those streets, SF Planning says, most blocks keep a four-story limit but lose the cap on how many homes fit inside it; corner lots and large lots can go to six stories. No one is required to build. Supporters say the west side must add housing or the state will take over zoning. Opponents say it invites speculation and does too little for renters, small shops and affordability. Neighborhoods United SF and Small Business Forward sued on January 9, 2026. This note found no later news on that suit.

Sources: [SF Planning, Family Zoning Plan](https://sfplanning.org/sf-family-zoning-plan), [KQED, Supervisors pass rezoning plan (December 2025)](https://www.kqed.org/news/12065708/san-francisco-supervisors-pass-rezoning-plan-making-way-for-taller-denser-housing), [SFist, Family zoning approved](https://sfist.com/2025/12/03/luries-family-zoning-approved-by-board-of-supervisors-handing-lurie-major-political-win), [KQED, Lawsuit over rezoning plan (January 2026)](https://www.kqed.org/news/12069226/neighborhood-small-business-groups-file-lawsuit-over-san-francisco-rezoning-plan).

## Sunset Boulevard greenbelt plans: irrigation, recycled water and a master plan

Sunset Boulevard's green strips run two miles from Golden Gate Park to Lake Merced, about 60 acres in all, and the city has a slow plan to revive them. San Francisco Public Works leads the Sunset Boulevard Master Plan, which aims for drought-tolerant planting, a healthier urban forest, more wildlife, and useful public spaces. The trouble has been water: then-Supervisor Joel Engardio blamed decades of drought, deferred upkeep and broken irrigation pipes for the worn look. On July 28, 2025 he and State Senator Scott Wiener announced $1.5 million: $1 million toward new irrigation fed by recycled water, and $500,000 for a maintenance and job training program tied to City College. Officials said the irrigation work would take at least two years. Public Works also lists a $900,000 irrigation repair and $2.4 million to connect SFPUC recycled water lines. The roadway itself was repaved and restriped by April 2025. The latest project news found was from November 2025.

Sources: [SF Public Works, Sunset Boulevard Master Plan](https://sfpublicworks.org/node/35112), [KALW, $1.5 million secured to revitalize Sunset Boulevard](https://www.kalw.org/bay-area-news/2025-07-29/1-5-million-secured-to-revitalize-sunset-boulevard), [Local News Matters, Re-greening of Sunset Boulevard](https://localnewsmatters.org/2025/07/29/re-greening-of-sfs-sunset-boulevard-takes-big-step-with-1-5m-in-state-and-local-funding/), [SFMTA, New data on Great Highway road closure](https://www.sfmta.com/blog/new-data-great-highway-road-closure-how-were-supporting-your-trips).
