This note answers three everyday questions: what parks are nearby, where to sit and read outside, and where a hammock can go. It lists every city park in and beside the Outer Sunset with its address and size, says what each has, and explains how fog and the afternoon west wind shape the day. It also sets out what the park rules do and do not say about tying things to trees. Sizes and counts come from the city's open data, read on November 17, 2026.

## What parks are in the Outer Sunset: the list, with addresses and acres

The city's Recreation and Parks Properties dataset lists these parks between Sunset Boulevard and the ocean, Lincoln Way and Sloat Boulevard:

- Sunset Dunes, the old Upper Great Highway from Lincoln Way to Sloat Boulevard: 50.6 acres.
- West Sunset Playground, 3223 Ortega Street, beside the Ortega library: 17.8 acres.
- South Sunset Playground, 2601 40th Avenue, near Vicente Street: 4.1 acres.

On the north edge is the west end of Golden Gate Park. The dataset splits the park into sections. The westernmost section, which holds the Beach Chalet, the Chain of Lakes and the Queen Wilhelmina Tulip Garden, is 212 acres. The next one east, with the Polo Field, Spreckels Lake and Lindley Meadow, is 224 acres.

Ocean Beach is federal land, part of Golden Gate National Recreation Area. The Sunset Boulevard greenbelt is about 60 acres of trees and lawn kept by Public Works, so it is not in the parks dataset. The San Francisco Zoo, just south of Sloat, is 131.5 acres.

Sources: [DataSF, Recreation and Parks Properties](https://data.sf.gov/resource/gtr9-ntp6.json?$select=property_name,acres,address&$where=latitude%20between%2037.715%20and%2037.775%20and%20longitude%20%3C%20-122.462&$order=longitude), [DataSF, Recreation and Parks Facilities](https://data.sf.gov/resource/ib5c-xgwu.json?$select=property_name,facility_type,facility_name&$where=property_name%20like%20%27Golden%20Gate%20Park%20-%20Section%206%27), [SF Recreation and Parks, Sunset Dunes Map (July 2025)](https://sfrecpark.org/DocumentCenter/View/26381/Sunset-Dunes-Map-July-2025), [SF Public Works, Sunset Boulevard Master Plan](https://sfpublicworks.org/node/35112).

## Hammocks and seats already built at Sunset Dunes, and exactly where

Sunset Dunes is the one nearby park with public hammocks. Recreation and Parks lists "lounge spaces with chairs, hammocks, and elevated seating for ocean views" among the park's features. Its July 2025 park map puts the hammock symbol in one place: beside a seating area just north of Taraval Street, between Santiago and Taraval.

The same map marks other seating areas near Judah and Kirkham, at Lawton by the Rock Spinner sculpture, at Noriega, and at Pacheco, where the logs are labeled Surf Perch Seating. The city's asset list adds detail: log benches at Judah, four log benches at Noriega, and memorial benches at Kirkham, Moraga, Ortega, Quintara (two), Santiago and Cutler.

These are free and need no straps or trees. The map is from July 2025 and the park is still being planned, so a piece may have moved. The seats face the ocean and the wind.

Sources: [SF Recreation and Parks, Sunset Dunes](https://sfrecpark.org/GreatHighwayPilot), [SF Recreation and Parks, Sunset Dunes Map (July 2025)](https://sfrecpark.org/DocumentCenter/View/26381/Sunset-Dunes-Map-July-2025), [DataSF, Assets Maintained by the Recreation and Parks Department](https://data.sf.gov/resource/e3jj-mbb3.json?$where=property_id=%27163%27).

## Calm places to read outside near the Outer Sunset, out of the west wind

No published list ranks reading spots in the Outer Sunset, so this section sticks to things you can check. The wind here comes from the west. Shelter means putting trees, a building or a hillside between you and the ocean.

- McCoppin Square, 24th Avenue and Taraval: the park's page describes a contemplative garden with seating and wooded paths. The Parkside library is on the same block.
- Pine Lake Park, Sloat and Vale: Recreation and Parks says it "sits in an elongated valley with steep, forested slopes," with a trail around the lake.
- Sunset Boulevard: the east side of the tree belt has thousands of mature cypress and pine between you and the sea.
- Golden Gate Park: the department says its gardens, groves and lakes offer spots for quiet reading. The Chain of Lakes is nearest.
- San Francisco Botanical Garden: its guide points to "tucked away benches" in the Temperate Asia collection.

Sunset Dunes has the most seats, but they are in the open.

Sources: [SF Recreation and Parks, McCoppin Square](https://www.sfrecpark.org/513/McCoppin-Square), [SF Recreation and Parks, Pine Lake Park](https://sfrecpark.org/facilities/facility/details/pinelakepark-197), [SF Recreation and Parks, Gardens, Groves and Lakes](https://sfrecpark.org/777/Gardens-Groves-and-Lakes), [Gardens of Golden Gate Park, San Francisco Botanical Garden](https://gggp.org/san-francisco-botanical-garden/), [DataSF, Street Tree Inventory, Sunset Boulevard](https://data.sf.gov/resource/tkzw-k3nq.json?$select=species,count%28*%29&$where=upper%28onstreet%29=%27SUNSET%20BLVD%27&$group=species&$order=count%28*%29%20DESC).

## Best time of day to read outside in the Outer Sunset, by season

Mornings are calmer and afternoons are windier. Meteorologist Jan Null explained to KQED that inland California heats up through the day while the ocean stays cold, and the cool air rushes toward the heat. Golden Gate Weather Services' climate summary says the summer sea breeze is strongest in the afternoon and evening, with west winds typically 20 to 30 miles per hour. It also says summer mornings start overcast and the ocean side clears only partly.

So in late spring and summer, the choice is between a still, gray morning and a brighter, windy afternoon. Recreation and Parks notes that the city's windiest months tend to be spring and early summer. The National Park Service says Ocean Beach is almost always in fog from late spring through summer, with temperatures in the 50s, and that September and October are the sunniest months. The climate summary agrees that spring and fall bring the most cloud-free days. Most of the rain falls from November through March.

Sources: [KQED, Why San Francisco gets so windy and foggy in the summer](https://www.kqed.org/news/11682511/why-san-francisco-gets-so-windy-and-foggy-in-the-summer), [Golden Gate Weather Services, Climate of San Francisco](https://www.ggweather.com/sf/narrative.html), [National Park Service, Ocean Beach](https://www.nps.gov/goga/planyourvisit/oceanbeach.htm), [SF Recreation and Parks, Sunset Dunes Fact Sheet, July to September 2026](https://sfrecpark.org/DocumentCenter/View/30380/Sunset-Dunes-Fact-Sheet_Q3_2026).

## San Francisco Botanical Garden: free for residents, and good for a book

The San Francisco Botanical Garden is in Golden Gate Park at 1199 9th Avenue, in the Inner Sunset. Admission is free for San Francisco residents. Bring a California license or ID with your city address, or a utility bill or lease plus photo ID. A photo of the document on your phone counts. For everyone else it is free daily from 7:30 to 9 a.m., on the second Tuesday of each month, and on Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day.

It opens daily at 7:30 a.m. Last entry is 4 p.m. from early November through January, 5 p.m. in February, early March and October, and 6 p.m. from mid-March through September. It closes an hour after last entry.

The garden's own guide mentions tucked-away benches in the Temperate Asia collection and invites picnics on the Great Meadow. Dogs are not allowed, except service animals. Sports gear and tree climbing are not allowed either, so leave the hammock at home.

Sources: [Gardens of Golden Gate Park, Get Tickets](https://gggp.org/tickets/), [Gardens of Golden Gate Park, Admissions and Hours](https://gggp.org/visit/admissions-hours/), [Gardens of Golden Gate Park, San Francisco Botanical Garden](https://gggp.org/san-francisco-botanical-garden/), [Gardens of Golden Gate Park, Frequently Asked Questions](https://gggp.org/visit/frequently-asked-questions/).

- Ortega Branch, 3223 Ortega Street, on the West Sunset Playground block. Public Works, which built it, describes "an adjacent outdoor terrace," a view of the Pacific Ocean, a green living roof and a playground next door. ## Little free libraries and other free book spots in the Sunset

Little free libraries are small boxes of books that neighbors build and stock: take one, leave one. The only published list found for the Sunset is from 7x7 in December 2017. It names three:

- 2618 45th Avenue, "the red little library right off 45th."
- Judah Street and 43rd Avenue.
- Lawton Street and 35th Avenue.

That list is nine years old. Boxes come and go, and none of the three was checked in person for this note. If you walk past one, a correction is welcome.

The Little Free Library organization wrote in 2024 that San Francisco's "Love Our Neighborhoods" ordinance made it easier for residents to put a box on the sidewalk. That followed a 2023 dispute in which a single complaint led the city to act against a well-loved box.

For a sure thing, the Ortega, Parkside and Sunset library branches lend books free with a library card.

## Can I hang a hammock or slackline in a San Francisco city park? What the Park Code says

No section of the Park Code that could be opened for this note names hammocks or slacklines. Two sections have been applied to them.

Section 4.01, "Disorderly Conduct," says no person shall, in any park without permission of the Recreation and Park Department, "(f) Climb or lie upon any tree, shrub, monument, wall, fence, shelter, fountain, statute, building, construction or structure." That wording is from the 2014 ordinance that last reprinted the section.

In April 2015, Mission Local reported that a man napping in a hammock between two trees in Dolores Park got a $192 citation under Section 4.05, which bars "malicious defacement, damage or destruction of real or personal property." He said his straps were tree-friendly and planned to contest it. The department did not tell the reporter whether hammocks are allowed.

So a hammock on park trees is not clearly permitted, and a ranger may cite it. The phrase "without permission" matters. Ask first: rpdinfo@sfgov.org or 415-831-2700.

Sources: [SF Board of Supervisors, Ordinance 72-14 (text of Park Code Sec. 4.01)](https://sfbos.org/ftp/uploadedfiles/bdsupvrs/ordinances14/o0072-14.pdf), [Mission Local, Dolores Park follies: malicious hammock (2015)](https://missionlocal.org/2015/04/dolores-park-follies-malicious-hammock/), [SF Recreation and Parks, Park Volunteers (contact details)](https://www.sfrecpark.org/1665/Park-Volunteers).

## Hammocks at Ocean Beach, Fort Funston and Lands End: national park rules

Ocean Beach, Fort Funston and Lands End belong to Golden Gate National Recreation Area, so city park rules do not apply there. The park's local rules are in the Superintendent's Compendium. The 2025 edition, signed in December 2025, was searched for this note. It does not contain the words hammock or slackline. It does have a section on preserving natural resources, which is where tree rules sit.

That silence is not permission. Another National Park Service site, Rock Creek Park, tells visitors that hammocks and slacklines "may not be tied to trees, shrubs, or any other natural or built feature," because ropes cut bark and the load can break limbs. It adds that any recreation that damages a natural resource is illegal, and it suggests free-standing frames on open grass.

In practice, Ocean Beach has sand and no trees, and Fort Funston is mostly open dune. Before tying to a tree on federal land, ask a ranger.

Sources: [National Park Service, Golden Gate NRA Superintendent's Compendium](https://www.nps.gov/goga/learn/management/superintendents-compendium.htm), [National Park Service, Rock Creek Park, Hug a Tree, Don't Hurt a Tree](https://www.nps.gov/rocr/planyourvisit/hug-a-tree-don-t-hurt-a-tree.htm), [National Park Service, Fort Funston](https://www.nps.gov/goga/planyourvisit/fortfunston.htm).

## How to hang a hammock without hurting a tree

Where a land manager allows a hammock, Leave No Trace publishes guidelines for doing it gently. The 2025 version says:

- Use webbing straps 1 inch wide or wider that go around the trunk. Never use anything thinner, and never hammer or screw anything into a tree.
- Wrap the strap once and leave it a little loose. A tight hang with several wraps can twist and crush the bark and the living layer under it.
- Pick living trees at least 6 inches in diameter, and hang from the thickest part of the trunk.
- Look up first for dead branches that could fall. Never use a dead tree.
- Hang less than 5 feet off the ground.
- Take the hammock down whenever you walk away.
- Check with the land manager first, since some parks prohibit hammocks.

The last point comes first in San Francisco, where the rules are unsettled. A free-standing hammock frame on open lawn avoids the tree question.

Sources: [Leave No Trace, Hammock Camping Guidelines (2025)](https://lnt.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2025-LNT-Hammock-Camping-Guidelines.pdf), [National Park Service, Rock Creek Park, Hug a Tree, Don't Hurt a Tree](https://www.nps.gov/rocr/planyourvisit/hug-a-tree-don-t-hurt-a-tree.htm).

## Which trees near the Outer Sunset are hammock-sized, and the limb-drop risk

The biggest rows of mature trees in the Outer Sunset are on Sunset Boulevard. The city's Street Tree Inventory has 3,515 records there. The most common are Monterey cypress (774, with an average recorded trunk diameter near 35 inches) and Monterey pine (637, about 24 inches). Those trunks are far thicker than the 6-inch minimum Leave No Trace gives. Stern Grove is, in a Recreation and Parks manager's words, "a eucalyptus grove," and the Greene family's eucalyptus still ring Pine Lake.

These same species fail. In January 2024 a nearly 100-foot eucalyptus uprooted in Golden Gate Park and landed on five cars. Arborist Frank Vasquez told ABC7 that eucalyptus are top-heavy with shallow roots. In October 2025 the city removed a 90-foot Monterey cypress at Hellman Hollow after its trunk split. The winter of 2023 took 140 park trees, and Stern Grove was hit hardest.

Look up before you sit under any of them, and stay out of the groves in wind or after rain.

Sources: [DataSF, Street Tree Inventory, Sunset Boulevard](https://data.sf.gov/resource/tkzw-k3nq.json?$select=species,count%28*%29&$where=upper%28onstreet%29=%27SUNSET%20BLVD%27&$group=species&$order=count%28*%29%20DESC), [ABC7, SF to inspect for hazards at Golden Gate Park](https://abc7news.com/post/sf-golden-gate-park-tree-fall-eucalyptus-arborist-trimming/14330788/), [SFist, Giant cypress taken down in Golden Gate Park](https://sfist.com/2025/10/30/sf-rec-parks-bids-fond-farewell-to-giant-cypress-that-had-to-be-taken-down-in-golden-gate-park), [CBS News Bay Area, Storms take toll on city park trees](https://www.cbsnews.com/sanfrancisco/news/january-23-storms-take-toll-on-sf-city-park-trees-golden-gate-stern-grove), [SF Recreation and Parks, Pine Lake Park](https://sfrecpark.org/facilities/facility/details/pinelakepark-197).

## Restrooms, water, wheelchair access and dog rules in Outer Sunset parks

Sunset Dunes has restrooms at Judah, Taraval and Sloat, open during the day, and a portable toilet at Noriega. Water fountains are beside the Judah and Taraval restrooms. The Judah and Taraval ramps are fully ADA accessible, with blue-zone parking on Lower Great Highway near Lincoln, Judah and Taraval.

City data lists restrooms at McCoppin Square, Parkside Square, Carl Larsen Park, Stern Grove and, in Golden Gate Park, at the Chain of Lakes and the Beach Chalet soccer fields. Pine Lake's page lists restrooms too. McCoppin Square has ADA-accessible paths. At Fort Funston the loop trails are paved and wheelchair-accessible. Beach wheelchairs can be requested at Ocean Beach.

Dogs must be leashed in city parks except in marked dog play areas. Stern Grove, Pine Lake and Golden Gate Park have them. At Sunset Dunes dogs stay leashed and off the dunes. On Ocean Beach, leashes are required from Stairwell 21 south to Sloat from July 1 to May 15, for the snowy plovers. The Botanical Garden does not allow dogs.

Sources: [Friends of Sunset Dunes, Visit](https://sunsetdunes.org/visit), [DataSF, Recreation and Parks Facilities](https://data.sf.gov/resource/ib5c-xgwu.json?$select=property_name,facility_name&$where=facility_type=%27Restroom%27&$limit=500), [SF Recreation and Parks, Dog Play Areas](https://sfrecpark.org/457/Dog-Play-Areas), [National Park Service, Ocean Beach](https://www.nps.gov/goga/planyourvisit/oceanbeach.htm), [National Park Service, Fort Funston](https://www.nps.gov/goga/planyourvisit/fortfunston.htm), [SF Recreation and Parks, Pine Lake Park](https://sfrecpark.org/facilities/facility/details/pinelakepark-197).

## How to volunteer in a park near the Outer Sunset, or start a friends group

Recreation and Parks welcomes drop-in volunteers any day of the week. The work is weeding, planting, mulching, trash pickup and trail care, plus habitat restoration in the wilder areas. One person or a group of up to nine picks a date from the department's volunteer calendar. Groups of ten or more fill out a request form and should allow up to five weeks. Questions go to recparkvolunteer@sfgov.org or 415-831-6884.

At Sunset Dunes, the nonprofit Friends of Sunset Dunes posts volunteer events and beach cleanups at lu.ma/sunsetdunes.

No formal adopt-a-park program was found. Neighbors who look after one park usually form a friends group. The San Francisco Parks Alliance, which held the money for about 80 such groups, folded in June 2025. The SF Standard reported in September 2026 that a new San Francisco Public Space Collective (sfpublicspace.org) now sponsors 15 of those groups and is building a network for 35 more. Others moved to Greening Projects or Livable City.

Sources: [SF Recreation and Parks, Park Volunteers](https://www.sfrecpark.org/1665/Park-Volunteers), [Friends of Sunset Dunes, Get Involved](https://sunsetdunes.org/get-involved), [SF Standard, Parks Alliance and the Public Space Collective (September 2026)](https://sfstandard.com/2026/09/19/san-francisco-nonprofit-parks-alliance-public-space-collective/).
