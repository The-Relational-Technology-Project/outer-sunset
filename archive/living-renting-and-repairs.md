This note is for a renter in the Outer Sunset (94122 and 94116, Supervisor District 4) whose landlord, property manager or super is not fixing things. It walks the usual ladder: ask in writing, follow up, call a city inspector, and use the Rent Board. It also covers what is particular to the avenues, where many rentals are houses, flats and in-law units, and where the rules differ from a big apartment building. It reports what the city's and the state's own pages say. It is not legal advice. A tenant counselor can tell you what fits your case, and the last sections say who they are.

## How to ask your landlord for a repair so there is a record

The San Francisco Rent Board's own advice is to start with a letter. List the problems you want fixed. The Rent Board says thirty days is considered reasonable for most repair work, and one or two days may be reasonable when the problem is critical, such as no heat in cold weather or a sewage backup.

If nothing happens, write a second letter. Say that as a last resort you may call a city inspector and may file a Rent Board petition if the items are not repaired by a date you think is reasonable. Keep copies of every letter and a log of phone calls and what was said.

The Housing Rights Committee of San Francisco adds two habits: follow any phone call with a note summing it up, and take photographs of the problem. This paper trail matters later. A Rent Board petition, an inspector's visit and a court case all turn on showing the landlord knew and had time to fix it.

Sources: [SF Rent Board, Handling Repair Problems](https://www.sf.gov/information--handling-repair-problems), [SF Rent Board, Resolving Problems With Your Landlord or Tenant](https://www.sf.gov/information--resolving-problems-your-landlord-or-tenant), [Housing Rights Committee of SF, Habitability and Repair Issues](https://www.hrcsf.org/habitability-repair-issues).

## How to file a housing complaint with the city (DBI) from the Sunset

Only a city inspector can order a landlord to make repairs. The Rent Board cannot. The inspectors work for Housing Inspection Services at the Department of Building Inspection (DBI).

There are three ways in. File online at DBI's complaint form. Call 311. Or call Housing Inspection Services at 628-652-3700 with questions and leave a voicemail with your street address, which is forwarded to the inspector for your area. On DBI's district map, the Sunset avenues checked for this note fall in Housing Inspection Services District 9. The map lists that inspector's name and direct phone, and those change, so look before you call.

DBI asks for the property address and the problem. Your name is optional, and you can ask to stay anonymous. Complaint records are public. DBI says it will send an inspector to investigate within 72 hours of a complaint.

Read the section on in-law units below before you call if your unit may not have permits.

Sources: [SF.gov, Report a building problem](https://www.sf.gov/report-building-problem), [SF.gov, Get help reporting substandard living conditions](https://www.sf.gov/get-help-reporting-substandard-living-conditions), [DBI online complaint form](https://dbiweb02.sfgov.org/dbi_complaints/default.aspx?page=AddressQuery), [DBI Housing Inspection Services district map](https://www.google.com/maps/d/u/0/viewer?ll=37.7705327165844%2C-122.4359665&z=13&mid=1IoVEN8COerHOTfiD7fLgWDYVimg), [SF.gov, Find a district inspector](https://www.sf.gov/information--find-district-inspector).

## What happens after the inspector comes: Notice of Violation and Director's Hearing

If the inspector finds a building or housing code violation, DBI writes a Notice of Violation, called an NOV. It describes the violation and the corrections the owner must make. It is posted on the building and mailed to the owner. While the case is open, DBI says the inspector follows up about every 30 days.

If the owner does not try to fix the problem, DBI moves the case to a Director's Hearing. The hearing officer can issue an Order of Abatement. Fees add up while the case stays open. An unfixed order can become a lien on the property, and DBI may refer an owner with more than three liens or orders to the City Attorney.

The Housing Rights Committee suggests asking the inspector for a copy of the NOV and keeping it. The Rent Board says an NOV is proof that there is a violation of law. You can follow the case by address or complaint number in DBI's tracking system.

Sources: [SF.gov, Fix your building and housing code violation](https://www.sf.gov/step-by-step--fix-your-building-and-housing-code-violation), [SF.gov, Notice of Violation (NOV)](https://www.sf.gov/information--notice-violation-nov), [SF.gov, Prepare for your Director's Hearing](https://www.sf.gov/prepare-your-directors-hearing-code-violation), [Housing Rights Committee of SF, Habitability and Repair Issues](https://www.hrcsf.org/habitability-repair-issues), [SF Rent Board, Failure to Repair and Maintain Petitions](https://www.sf.gov/information--failure-repair-and-maintain-petitions).

## How warm does my landlord have to keep my apartment in San Francisco?

The Rent Board's summary of the San Francisco Housing Code says heat is an essential service that a landlord must provide to all tenants. In every rental unit except hotel rooms, there must be a permanent heat source able to keep rooms at 70 degrees Fahrenheit, measured three feet above the floor, in all habitable rooms except bathrooms and hallways. When the tenant does not control the heat, the landlord must provide it 24 hours a day. Handing out portable space heaters does not meet the rule.

The morning and evening hours people often quote, 5 to 11 AM and 3 to 10 PM at 68 degrees, apply to residential hotel rooms, not to houses, flats and apartments.

For hot water, California Civil Code 1941.1 lists a water supply that produces hot and cold running water among the basics of a livable home.

If the heat is out, the Rent Board says to contact a DBI building inspector, who can issue a Notice of Violation.

Sources: [SF Rent Board, Minimum Heat Requirements](https://www.sf.gov/information--minimum-heat-requirements), [California Civil Code section 1941.1](https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_1941.1).

## Mold, mice, bed bugs, garbage, locks and elevators: who is responsible?

California Civil Code 1941.1 lists what a rental must have. The list includes weather protection of roof, walls, windows and doors, working plumbing, gas, heating and electrical systems, floors and stairs in good repair, enough garbage bins, and areas under the landlord's control kept free of garbage, rodents and vermin. Civil Code 1941.3 requires a working deadbolt on each main entry door and locks on most windows that open.

The San Francisco Department of Public Health says property owners must make sure their property does not have vermin, and must answer a tenant's bed bug complaint by hiring a licensed pest control operator. It also says owners and tenants share responsibility for preventing infestations. Report pests through 311. The Healthy Housing Program is at 415-252-3800.

The Housing Rights Committee says mold and mildew fall under the Health Code and can be reported to the Health Department. The Rent Board counts elevator service, trash removal, heat, water and repairs as housing services.

Sources: [California Civil Code section 1941.1](https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_1941.1), [California Civil Code section 1941.3](https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_1941.3), [SF.gov, Keeping your building free of vermin](https://www.sf.gov/information--keeping-your-building-free-vermin), [SF.gov, Get help with vermin in your building](https://www.sf.gov/get-help-vermin-your-building), [Housing Rights Committee of SF, Habitability and Repair Issues](https://www.hrcsf.org/habitability-repair-issues), [SF Rent Board, Housing services](https://www.sf.gov/information--housing-services).

## Asking the Rent Board for lower rent when repairs are not made (decrease in housing services)

A tenant in a rent controlled unit can petition the Rent Board for a rent reduction when the landlord has substantially decreased a housing service without lowering the rent. Repairs and maintenance count. The Rent Board says this petition is not available to most tenants in units built after June 13, 1979.

The form is 516A, also offered in Chinese, Spanish, Filipino and Vietnamese. Filing is free. You list each lost service, when it stopped, when you told the landlord, and how much you think the rent should drop. Email it with your evidence to rentboard@sfgov.org or bring it to 25 Van Ness Avenue, Suite 700.

Usually within a few months the case goes to mediation, then to arbitration if that fails. Reductions generally reach back only one year before filing. The Rent Board can lower the rent. It cannot order the repair.

In the year ending October 31, 2026, the city's open data shows 117 Rent Board petitions from Sunset/Parkside, 30 of them citing decreased housing services.

Sources: [SF Rent Board, Request a rent reduction for decreased housing services](https://www.sf.gov/request-rent-reduction-decreased-housing-services), [SF Rent Board, Tenant Petitions](https://www.sf.gov/tenant-petitions), [DataSF, Petitions to the Rent Board (6swy-cmkq)](https://data.sf.gov/d/6swy-cmkq).

## Putting off a rent increase until repairs are done, and the Rent Board counseling line

A second petition, Failure to Repair and Maintain (form 516B), asks the Rent Board to postpone an annual rent increase until requested repairs are made. It must be filed within 60 days of receiving the rent increase notice. The tenant has to show that the landlord knew the work was needed and that the law required it, so ask in writing and request a DBI inspection.

The Rent Board says to keep paying the disputed increase until the decision comes. If the tenant wins, the landlord is ordered to refund it. It can be combined with a decrease in services claim.

Rent Board counselors answer the phone at 415-252-4600, Monday through Friday, 9 AM to noon and 1 to 4 PM. Calls are generally limited to five minutes. Drop-in counseling is at 25 Van Ness Avenue, Suite 700, weekdays 9 AM to 4 PM. It is free, in English, Spanish, Cantonese and Mandarin. Counselors cannot give legal advice.

Sources: [SF Rent Board, Failure to Repair and Maintain Petitions](https://www.sf.gov/information--failure-repair-and-maintain-petitions), [SF Rent Board, Speak with a Rent Board counselor](https://www.sf.gov/speak-rent-board-counselor).

## Can I fix it myself and take it off the rent? What California law says

California Civil Code 1942 is the "repair and deduct" law. If a landlord does not fix conditions that make a home untenantable within a reasonable time after notice, the tenant may make the repair and deduct the cost from rent, when the cost is not more than one month's rent. It cannot be used more than twice in any 12 months. Acting after the 30th day following notice is presumed reasonable.

The city is cautious about this. The Rent Board says the Rent Ordinance itself does not authorize withholding rent or repair and deduct, that these state remedies "pose risks and can lead to an eviction action," and that a tenant should get legal advice first. The Housing Rights Committee recommends getting the landlord's agreement in writing before deducting.

Another route for money already paid is Small Claims Court. The limit for an individual is $12,500, lawyers do not appear, the filing fee is $30 to $100, and a trial date is typically one to two months out.

Sources: [California Civil Code section 1942](https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_1942), [SF Rent Board, Handling Repair Problems](https://www.sf.gov/information--handling-repair-problems), [SF Rent Board, Failure to Repair and Maintain Petitions](https://www.sf.gov/information--failure-repair-and-maintain-petitions), [Housing Rights Committee of SF, Habitability and Repair Issues](https://www.hrcsf.org/habitability-repair-issues), [California Courts Self-Help Guide, Small claims](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/small-claims-california), [SF Superior Court, Small Claims](https://sf.courts.ca.gov/divisions/small-claims).

## Can my landlord punish me for complaining, or walk in without notice?

California Civil Code 1942.5 covers retaliation. If a tenant who is current on rent complains to the landlord or to an agency about livability, the landlord may not, in retaliation, evict, force the tenant out, raise the rent or cut services within 180 days. Threatening to report a tenant to immigration authorities counts as retaliation. Tenants who join a tenants' association are also protected.

Civil Code 1954 covers entry. A landlord may enter only for listed reasons, such as an emergency, necessary or agreed repairs, or showing the unit. Written notice is required, 24 hours is presumed reasonable, and entry is during normal business hours unless the tenant agrees. The Rent Board notes that unreasonably refusing access can itself be grounds for eviction.

San Francisco's Rent Ordinance section 37.10B bars a landlord from failing in bad faith to make required repairs or provide required services, and from abusing the right of entry. The Rent Board does not rule on harassment or retaliation claims as such. Those go to court.

Sources: [California Civil Code section 1942.5](https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_1942.5), [California Civil Code section 1954](https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_1954), [SF Rent Board, Landlord Access to a Unit](https://www.sf.gov/information--landlord-access-unit), [SF Rent Board, Sec. 37.10B Tenant Harassment](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-3710b-tenant-harassment), [SF Rent Board, Tenant Petitions](https://www.sf.gov/tenant-petitions).

## Is my Sunset house, flat or in-law unit under rent control?

It depends on the building, and this decides whether the Rent Board petitions above are open to you. The Rent Board says many units built on or before June 13, 1979 have both rent control and eviction protection. You can check the year your building was built on the Property Information Map.

Single-family homes and condos are different. Under the state Costa-Hawkins law they are generally exempt from the rent increase limits, but not from just cause eviction rules, if the tenancy began on or after January 1, 1996. A house with an in-law unit, legal or not, counts as a two-unit building and is not exempt, unless both units are rented together as one tenancy.

This matters on the avenues. In the Rent Board's Housing Inventory open data for Sunset/Parkside, about 46 percent of the units owners reported for 2026 were in one-unit or two-unit properties. That data is owner-reported and unverified.

Some units are hard to classify. The Rent Board suggests asking a counselor.

Sources: [SF Rent Board, Learn about San Francisco rental laws](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-san-francisco-rental-laws), [SF Rent Board, Partial Exemption for Certain Single-Family Homes and Condominiums Under Costa-Hawkins](https://www.sf.gov/information--partial-exemption-certain-single-family-homes-and-condominiums-under-costa-hawkins), [SF Planning, Property Information Map](https://sfplanninggis.org/pim/), [DataSF, Rent Board Housing Inventory (gdc7-dmcn)](https://data.sf.gov/d/gdc7-dmcn).

## I live in an in-law unit that may not have permits. Is it safe to call the inspector?

This is a real tradeoff. The Rent Board states that a unit without permits is still covered by the Rent Ordinance. It also warns that when a tenant in such a unit calls DBI, the inspector might cite the owner for the unit itself, and the unit may have to be removed. Removing a unit is a just cause for eviction, but the landlord must first get all the permits and must pay relocation money.

The Housing Rights Committee and the San Francisco Tenants Union both advise such tenants not to call DBI first. They suggest a Rent Board petition or a counselor instead. Signs the Housing Rights Committee lists: a garage, basement or attic unit, an address with "A" or "1/2", or no mailbox of your own.

The city has a Unit Legalization Program for units that existed before January 1, 2013. Improvement costs cannot be passed on to tenants, and an existing Notice of Violation can be suspended for a year while the owner legalizes.

Sources: [SF Rent Board, Renting an Unpermitted Unit](https://www.sf.gov/information--renting-unpermitted-unit), [SF Rent Board, Evictions to Demolish or Permanently Remove an Individual Unit From Housing Use](https://www.sf.gov/information--evictions-demolish-or-permanently-remove-unit-housing-use), [Housing Rights Committee of SF, Habitability and Repair Issues](https://www.hrcsf.org/habitability-repair-issues), [San Francisco Tenants Union, Repairs](https://sftu.org/repairs/), [SF.gov, Check the feasibility of legalizing an illegal unit in your home](https://www.sf.gov/check-feasibility-legalizing-illegal-unit-your-home), [SF.gov, Legalize a unit in your home](https://www.sf.gov/step-by-step--legalize-unit-your-home).

## Organizing with the neighbors in your building: San Francisco's tenant right-to-organize law

When one unit has repair problems, the others often do too. The Tenants Union and the Housing Rights Committee both say tenants who write, call the inspector and petition together are stronger than one tenant alone.

In 2022 the city strengthened the law behind this, Administrative Code Chapter 49A. The Rent Board calls it the Tenant Right-To-Organize legislation. It took effect April 11, 2022. Landlords must allow organizing in common areas, including door-to-door surveys and outside advocates.

In buildings with five or more rental units, tenants can form a Tenant Association with the approval of a majority of occupied units. The landlord must then meet and confer in good faith and, on written request, attend a meeting every three months. Refusing can support a rent reduction petition. Many Sunset buildings have fewer than five units, so those association rules may not reach them.

The Housing Rights Committee says it may be able to help tenants organize and form an association.

Sources: [SF Rent Board, New legislation on tenant organizing and tenant associations](https://www.sf.gov/news--new-legislation-tenant-organizing-and-tenant-associations), [San Francisco Tenants Union, Right-To-Organize Passed by SF Supervisors](https://sftu.org/?p=12482), [San Francisco Tenants Union, Repairs](https://sftu.org/repairs/), [Housing Rights Committee of SF, Habitability and Repair Issues](https://www.hrcsf.org/habitability-repair-issues).

## How to look up a building's complaint and permit history

Before you write the letter, or before you sign a lease, you can see what the city already knows about an address.

DBI's Permit/Complaint Tracking System (dbiweb02.sfgov.org/dbipts) searches by address or complaint number and shows complaints and permits. SF Planning's Property Information Map (sfplanninggis.org/pim) shows the year a building was built.

The city also publishes the raw records on DataSF. All four datasets below showed an update on November 17, 2026. DBI Complaints is dataset gm2e-bten. Notices of Violation is nbtm-fbw5. Petitions to the Rent Board is 6swy-cmkq, with addresses masked to the block. Rent Board Housing Inventory is gdc7-dmcn. Each can be filtered to the "Sunset/Parkside" neighborhood or to Supervisor District 4.

A count run for this note: from November 1, 2025 through October 31, 2026, Housing Inspection Services received 338 complaints in Sunset/Parkside, and 138 of them show a first Notice of Violation. Complaint text is public, which is worth knowing before you file one.

Sources: [DBI Permit/Complaint Tracking System](https://dbiweb02.sfgov.org/dbipts/), [SF Planning, Property Information Map](https://sfplanninggis.org/pim/), [DataSF, Department of Building Inspection Complaints (gm2e-bten)](https://data.sf.gov/d/gm2e-bten), [DataSF, Notices of Violation issued by DBI (nbtm-fbw5)](https://data.sf.gov/d/nbtm-fbw5), [DataSF, Petitions to the Rent Board (6swy-cmkq)](https://data.sf.gov/d/6swy-cmkq), [DataSF, Rent Board Housing Inventory (gdc7-dmcn)](https://data.sf.gov/d/gdc7-dmcn), [SF.gov, Report a building problem](https://www.sf.gov/report-building-problem).

## If your landlord is also your neighbor: mediation before escalation

On the avenues the landlord is often the family upstairs. If you want the repair and also want to stay on good terms, mediation is an option before inspectors and petitions.

Community Boards has run neighborhood mediation in San Francisco since 1976. Mediation is voluntary and confidential. Trained volunteer mediators do not take sides or decide who is right. You call first, staff then invite the other person, and it takes about 10 to 15 business days from opening a case to the session, held at the Opera Plaza office or on Zoom. The mediation itself is free. There is a $50 to $100 case opening fee, on a sliding scale or waived if you cannot pay. Community Boards says it does not collect immigration status.

The Rent Board also has an Alternative Dispute Resolution program for housing conflicts between tenants, landlords, roommates and neighbors, requested on its ADR form. It is not available while a Rent Board petition or court case on the same issue is pending.

Sources: [Community Boards, Neighborhood Mediation](https://communityboards.org/resolution-services/neighborhood/), [Community Boards, FAQ](https://communityboards.org/about/faq/), [SF Rent Board, Resolving Problems With Your Landlord or Tenant](https://www.sf.gov/information--resolving-problems-your-landlord-or-tenant).
