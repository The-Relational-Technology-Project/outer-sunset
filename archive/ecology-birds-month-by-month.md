This note is the calendar and the map for the question "what is around today?" It says which birds a neighbor can expect in each stretch of the year in the avenues' yards, on Ocean Beach, on the park lakes and Lake Merced, and past the breakers. It lists the exact places birders report from, the pages that show today's sightings, the best time of day and tide, the common voices outside a Sunset window, and how to start. Most of the calendar comes from eBird's month-by-month lists for the local hotspots, read on November 17, 2026.

## What birds are around the Outer Sunset in mid to late November: yards, wires and park trees

By mid-November the winter birds are in. In yards and street trees, look for white-crowned and golden-crowned sparrows on the ground, yellow-rumped warblers moving through with a loud chip, and Townsend's warblers, yellow and black on the face, up in cypress and pine. SF Environment calls those two San Francisco's winter warblers. At North Lake, eBird shows golden-crowned sparrow, fox sparrow, hermit thrush, ruby-crowned kinglet, Townsend's warbler and yellow-rumped warbler recorded in each of the last ten Novembers. At Sunset Reservoir the November regulars are white-crowned sparrow, junco, both warblers, ruby-crowned kinglet and hermit thrush, with black-bellied plovers and killdeer found there most years. Along Sunset Dunes, Golden Gate Bird Alliance's 2025 to 2026 survey found Say's phoebes and western meadowlarks wintering in the dunes. The year-round neighbors (Anna's hummingbird, black phoebe, house finch, California towhee, crow, raven) are all still here. Project FeederWatch, a November to April count, needs only a spot to watch from.

Sources: [eBird, North Lake, November birds](https://ebird.org/region/L370768/iconic-birds?m=11), [eBird, Sunset Reservoir, November birds](https://ebird.org/region/L1281411/iconic-birds?m=11), [Golden Gate Bird Alliance, Birds of Sunset Dunes (2026)](https://goldengatebirdalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Birds-of-Sunset-Dunes_Final.pdf), [SF Environment, Fall Migration](https://www.sfenvironment.org/blog/fall-migration), [Project FeederWatch, overview](https://feederwatch.org/about/project-overview/).

## Ducks, grebes and marsh birds on the Chain of Lakes and Lake Merced in November

Ducks from the north are on the lakes by November. At North Lake, eBird shows ring-necked ducks in each of the last ten Novembers, ruddy ducks in nine, hooded mergansers in eight, northern shovelers in seven and buffleheads in six. Mallards, coots, pied-billed grebes and black-crowned night herons are there every year. SF Environment's David Assmann wrote that hooded mergansers can almost always be found in Golden Gate Park in winter, often at Lloyd Lake, and that American wigeon winter at Lloyd Lake too.

Lake Merced is bigger water with reeds. At the concrete bridge the every-November birds are ruddy duck, western, Clark's, eared and pied-billed grebes, coot, double-crested cormorant, marsh wren, common yellowthroat and great-tailed grackle. Virginia rail and sora, two shy marsh birds, are recorded most Novembers. By November 17, 2026, birders had logged 80 species there for the month, among them cinnamon teal, American wigeon and northern shoveler.

Sources: [eBird, North Lake, November birds](https://ebird.org/region/L370768/iconic-birds?m=11), [eBird, Lake Merced concrete bridge, November birds](https://ebird.org/region/L791371/iconic-birds?m=11), [eBird, Lake Merced concrete bridge, this month's list](https://ebird.org/region/L791371/bird-list?yr=curM), [SF Environment, Wood Ducks and Sea Ducks](https://sfenvironment.org/node/2950), [SF Environment, More On Ducks](https://sfenvironment.org/node/2951).

## Ocean Beach in November: plovers and sanderlings on the sand, scoters, loons and grebes past the breakers

On the sand in November, sanderlings and snowy plovers are certain. eBird has both in each of the last ten Novembers at Ocean Beach, with long-billed curlew in eight and marbled godwit in six. This November's list also has black-bellied plover, dunlin, willet and whimbrel. Gulls are at their most varied: western, California, Heermann's, short-billed and glaucous-winged every year.

Look past the breakers for surf scoters, a ten-for-ten November bird, along with red-throated and Pacific loons, western grebes and brown pelicans. Common loons and Brandt's cormorants show in nine years of ten. Black scoters, white-winged scoters and ancient murrelets turn up in about half of Novembers. SF Environment notes the rarer scoters stay well offshore, so bring a scope. A second eBird hotspot, across from the Oceanside treatment plant, is kept for birders who stand still and scan the sea. This November's Ocean Beach list also has northern fulmar, sooty shearwater and two kinds of jaeger.

Sources: [eBird, Ocean Beach, November birds](https://ebird.org/region/L302090/iconic-birds?m=11), [eBird, Ocean Beach, this month's list](https://ebird.org/region/L302090/bird-list?yr=curM), [eBird, Ocean Beach seawatch, November birds](https://ebird.org/region/L1022599/iconic-birds?m=11), [SF Environment, Wood Ducks and Sea Ducks](https://sfenvironment.org/node/2950).

## Varied thrushes, waxwings, crossbills and lost warblers: irruption years and late-fall rarities

Some winter birds come in numbers only in certain years. SF Environment explains that the Christmas Bird Count usually finds about a dozen varied thrushes in the city, but found 150 in 2006. That is an irruption: food runs short to the north and birds push south. Alan Hopkins's Lands End checklist marks varied thrush, pine siskin and red crossbill as "eruptive" for the same reason. Cedar waxwings and pine siskins appear at North Lake in about half of Novembers.

November 2026 looks like one of those years. By November 17, eBird's Golden Gate Park list for the month had varied thrush, cedar waxwing, pine siskin, red-breasted nuthatch and red crossbill, plus a Lewis's woodpecker and a Clark's nutcracker. Crossbills were also at Sunset Reservoir and Lake Merced. Late fall brings lost eastern birds too. This month's park list has Tennessee, magnolia and black-and-white warblers and a summer tanager. All told, San Francisco County's eBird list stands at 517 species, 211 of them seen this month.

Sources: [SF Environment, Warblers, Woodpeckers and Vagrants](https://sfenvironment.org/node/2974), [Golden Gate National Parks Conservancy, Birds of Lands End brochure (Alan Hopkins, 2007)](https://www.parksconservancy.org/sites/default/files/Lands_End_Birds_Brochure.pdf), [eBird, Golden Gate Park, this month's list](https://ebird.org/region/L109439/bird-list?yr=curM), [eBird, Sunset Reservoir, this month's list](https://ebird.org/region/L1281411/bird-list?yr=curM), [eBird, San Francisco County bird list](https://ebird.org/region/US-CA-075/bird-list).

## Birds in December and January: the full winter cast, and the Christmas count

December and January are November with everything settled in. At North Lake, ring-necked duck, ruddy duck, golden-crowned sparrow, fox sparrow, hermit thrush, ruby-crowned kinglet and both winter warblers are recorded every December and January, buffleheads in nine or ten years of ten, and hooded mergansers in every January. On Ocean Beach, snowy plovers, sanderlings, surf scoters and red-throated loons are constant, black scoters show in nine years of ten, and Say's phoebes hunt the dunes in eight Decembers of ten. At Lake Merced, soras are recorded every December, and tree swallows are over the water every January.

Two things mark the season. The San Francisco Christmas Bird Count is Monday, December 28, 2026, and Golden Gate Bird Alliance's page lists a November training for newcomers. And the first spring migrant can arrive before winter ends: Allen's hummingbird has been at North Lake in eight of the last ten Januaries.

Sources: [eBird, North Lake, December birds](https://ebird.org/region/L370768/iconic-birds?m=12), [eBird, North Lake, January birds](https://ebird.org/region/L370768/iconic-birds?m=1), [eBird, Ocean Beach, December birds](https://ebird.org/region/L302090/iconic-birds?m=12), [eBird, Lake Merced concrete bridge, January birds](https://ebird.org/region/L791371/iconic-birds?m=1), [Golden Gate Bird Alliance, Christmas Bird Counts](https://goldengatebirdalliance.org/birding-resources/christmas-bird-counts/).

## Birds in February and March: Allen's hummingbirds and swallows arrive, ducks get ready to go

Spring starts early here. Allen's hummingbird, the small orange and green one, is recorded at North Lake and Lake Merced in every February of the last ten. Tree swallows are regular over North Lake by February. In March barn, violet-green and rough-winged swallows join them at Lake Merced, and the first Wilson's warblers arrive. Cedar waxwing flocks are a March regular at North Lake.

The winter birds are still here. SF Environment calls February a great month for dabbling ducks on the park lakes (Lloyd, North, Elk Glen) and warns that by early March they start heading north. Ring-necked ducks stay at North Lake through March. On Ocean Beach, whimbrels, willets and marbled godwits work the wet sand with the sanderlings.

The Great Backyard Bird Count runs February 12 to 15, 2027. Audubon says to count for as little as 15 minutes and report at birdcount.org. Golden Gate Bird Alliance treats February 1 through August 31 as nesting season, a time for extra care with yard work.

Sources: [eBird, North Lake, February birds](https://ebird.org/region/L370768/iconic-birds?m=2), [eBird, North Lake, March birds](https://ebird.org/region/L370768/iconic-birds?m=3), [eBird, Lake Merced concrete bridge, March birds](https://ebird.org/region/L791371/iconic-birds?m=3), [eBird, Ocean Beach, March birds](https://ebird.org/region/L302090/iconic-birds?m=3), [SF Environment, More On Ducks](https://sfenvironment.org/node/2951), [Audubon, Great Backyard Bird Count](https://www.audubon.org/community-science/great-backyard-bird-count), [Golden Gate Bird Alliance, Birds of Sunset Dunes (2026)](https://goldengatebirdalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Birds-of-Sunset-Dunes_Final.pdf).

## Birds in April and May: shorebirds heading north, winter birds leaving, nesters singing

April is the busiest month on Ocean Beach. Shorebirds bound for the north stop to feed: western sandpipers, dunlin, short-billed dowitchers, semipalmated plovers, whimbrels, godwits and curlews are all recorded in nine or ten Aprils of ten. Offshore, all three loons pass, and Caspian terns, pigeon guillemots and common murres are regular. In May the snowy plovers leave. Golden Gate Bird Alliance gives their absence as about May 15 to July 1. Bank swallows are on the Ocean Beach list every May.

In the park and yards the trade is plain to see. Golden-crowned sparrows, Townsend's and yellow-rumped warblers are still regulars at North Lake in April and drop off its May list. Ring-necked ducks and buffleheads are last regular in April. In their place come Wilson's warblers, three kinds of swallow, and in May warbling vireos and western tanagers. At Lake Merced, cliff swallows and green herons are back in April. BirdCast's spring migration radar is live March 1 to June 15.

Sources: [eBird, Ocean Beach, April birds](https://ebird.org/region/L302090/iconic-birds?m=4), [eBird, Ocean Beach, May birds](https://ebird.org/region/L302090/iconic-birds?m=5), [eBird, North Lake, April birds](https://ebird.org/region/L370768/iconic-birds?m=4), [eBird, North Lake, May birds](https://ebird.org/region/L370768/iconic-birds?m=5), [eBird, Lake Merced concrete bridge, April birds](https://ebird.org/region/L791371/iconic-birds?m=4), [Golden Gate Bird Alliance, Birds of Sunset Dunes (2026)](https://goldengatebirdalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Birds-of-Sunset-Dunes_Final.pdf), [BirdCast, Migration Dashboard guide](https://birdcast.org/migration-tools/migration-dashboard/).

## Birds in June and July: the quiet months, fledglings, and the first birds back on the beach

June is the quiet month. Ocean Beach's list of regulars is its shortest of the year, and most birds around are residents raising young. Around Sunset Reservoir the June birds are house sparrow, mockingbird, white-crowned sparrow, junco, house finch and California towhee. Golden Gate Bird Alliance notes white-crowned sparrow parents feeding fledged young in spring at Sunset Dunes, and western bluebird families there in spring and summer. At North Lake, Allen's hummingbirds, Wilson's warblers, purple finches, tree, barn and violet-green swallows, Cooper's hawks and pied-billed grebes are recorded nearly every June and July. Lake Merced adds bank and cliff swallows, green herons, Caspian terns and western and Clark's grebes.

Ocean Beach in June is Heermann's gulls, brown pelicans, Caspian terns, murres offshore and whimbrels. Then July turns the calendar. Snowy plovers, sanderlings, marbled godwits and western sandpipers are back in every July of the last ten, and elegant terns appear.

Sources: [eBird, Sunset Reservoir, June birds](https://ebird.org/region/L1281411/iconic-birds?m=6), [eBird, North Lake, June birds](https://ebird.org/region/L370768/iconic-birds?m=6), [eBird, Lake Merced concrete bridge, June birds](https://ebird.org/region/L791371/iconic-birds?m=6), [eBird, Ocean Beach, June birds](https://ebird.org/region/L302090/iconic-birds?m=6), [eBird, Ocean Beach, July birds](https://ebird.org/region/L302090/iconic-birds?m=7), [Golden Gate Bird Alliance, Birds of Sunset Dunes (2026)](https://goldengatebirdalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Birds-of-Sunset-Dunes_Final.pdf).

## Birds in August: fall migration starts while it is still foggy

Fall migration begins in August, weeks before it feels like fall. At North Lake the August list fills with birds passing through: western flycatcher in every August of the last ten, yellow warbler and warbling vireo in nine, willow flycatcher and western tanager in eight. Belted kingfishers are back on the lake every August.

The ocean is at its liveliest. From Ocean Beach, eBird has elegant terns, parasitic jaegers, surf scoters and pigeon guillemots in every August, with pomarine jaegers, ospreys and red-necked phalaropes in nine years and sooty shearwaters in eight. Snowy plovers, sanderlings, godwits, whimbrels and western sandpipers are on the sand. Red-necked phalaropes also turn up on Lake Merced in August and September.

BirdCast's fall radar for San Francisco County goes live August 1. Golden Gate Bird Alliance counts nesting season as running through August 31.

Sources: [eBird, North Lake, August birds](https://ebird.org/region/L370768/iconic-birds?m=8), [eBird, Ocean Beach, August birds](https://ebird.org/region/L302090/iconic-birds?m=8), [eBird, Lake Merced concrete bridge, August birds](https://ebird.org/region/L791371/iconic-birds?m=8), [BirdCast, Migration Dashboard guide](https://birdcast.org/migration-tools/migration-dashboard/), [Golden Gate Bird Alliance, Birds of Sunset Dunes (2026)](https://goldengatebirdalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Birds-of-Sunset-Dunes_Final.pdf).

## Birds in September and October: peak migration, then the winter birds return

September is the month to walk the Chain of Lakes slowly. At North Lake, western wood-pewee, willow flycatcher, yellow warbler, western tanager and warbling vireo are recorded in every September of the last ten, and the first Townsend's warblers return. Even a rarity, the chestnut-sided warbler, has turned up there in eight Septembers of ten. Black-bellied plovers are back at Sunset Reservoir.

October is the handoff. Ruby-crowned kinglets, hermit thrushes, fox sparrows and yellow-rumped warblers are on every October list at North Lake, and ring-necked and ruddy ducks return to the water. At Lake Merced, Virginia rails, soras and northern shovelers come back. Off Ocean Beach, elegant terns and jaegers linger while loons return and dunlin arrive on the sand.

Overhead, hawks are moving. SF Environment says that on a good September or October day more than 500 raptors of up to 19 species are counted from Hawk Hill in the Marin Headlands, and many cross to San Francisco.

Sources: [eBird, North Lake, September birds](https://ebird.org/region/L370768/iconic-birds?m=9), [eBird, North Lake, October birds](https://ebird.org/region/L370768/iconic-birds?m=10), [eBird, Lake Merced concrete bridge, October birds](https://ebird.org/region/L791371/iconic-birds?m=10), [eBird, Ocean Beach, October birds](https://ebird.org/region/L302090/iconic-birds?m=10), [eBird, Sunset Reservoir, September birds](https://ebird.org/region/L1281411/iconic-birds?m=9), [SF Environment, Fall Migration](https://www.sfenvironment.org/blog/fall-migration).

## Best time of day and tide to see birds: rising tide, dawn song, dusk crows, owls at night

On the beach, watch the tide. Audubon's guide says a rising mid-tide pushes shorebirds up the beach toward you, starting about three hours before high tide. At high tide they rest, and should be left alone. As the water drops they fan out to feed. Golden Gate Bird Alliance adds that many shorebirds feed hard just as the high tide goes out, and that tides run most extreme in December and January. Search "NOAA Tide Predictions" for the day's chart.

For songbirds, go early. BirdNote puts the dawn chorus just before sunrise. Kenn Kaufman writes that in spring it starts at the first hint of light and fades once the sun is up. SF Environment notes wood ducks at Lake Merced usually show only in the very early morning.

Around sunset in winter, KQED reports, crows flock to the same roosts night after night. After dark, listen for the great horned owl's low hoo, hoo-hoo, hoo, hoo. Barn owls hunt Sunset Dunes at night.

Sources: [Audubon, How to Read the Tides for Shorebird Photography](https://www.audubon.org/magazine/how-read-tides-shorebird-photography), [Golden Gate Bird Alliance, Wintering Shorebirds](https://goldengatebirdalliance.org/blog-posts/wintering-shorebirds/), [BirdNote, Which Species of Bird Sings First in the Morning?](https://birdnote.org/podcasts/birdnote-daily/which-species-bird-sings-first-morning), [Audubon, Ask Kenn Kaufman](https://www.audubon.org/magazine/ask-kenn-kaufman-which-birds-are-most-their-dinosaur-ancestors), [SF Environment, Wood Ducks and Sea Ducks](https://sfenvironment.org/node/2950), [KQED, Is it crow-maggedon?](https://www.kqed.org/news/12072433/is-it-crow-maggedon-why-crows-are-flocking-to-bay-area-cities-each-winter), [Audubon, Great Horned Owl](https://www.audubon.org/field-guide/bird/great-horned-owl), [Golden Gate Bird Alliance, Birds of Sunset Dunes (2026)](https://goldengatebirdalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Birds-of-Sunset-Dunes_Final.pdf).

## Birding by ear from a Sunset window: the voices heard most

Most birds outside a Sunset window are heard before they are seen. These descriptions are Audubon's.

- White-crowned sparrow: a short series of clear whistles, then buzzy notes.
- Golden-crowned sparrow, in winter: three sad notes sliding down, like "oh, dear me".
- Anna's hummingbird: a sharp chip and a rapid, scratchy chee-chee-chee.
- House finch: a long, lively warble ending in a zeee, often from an antenna.
- Dark-eyed junco: a ringing trill on one pitch.
- Black phoebe: a thin, buzzy pi-tsee, repeated, and a sharp chip.
- California towhee: squeaky chips that speed up into a trill, and a sharp chink.
- Mourning dove: a low, mournful coo-ah, coo, coo, coo.
- Crow and raven: the crow's caw-caw against the raven's deep croak, a hollow wonk-wonk.
- Yellow-rumped warbler, in winter: a sharp chek from the trees.

After dark, low hoots in a series are a great horned owl.

Sources: [Audubon, White-crowned Sparrow](https://www.audubon.org/field-guide/bird/white-crowned-sparrow), [Audubon, Golden-crowned Sparrow](https://www.audubon.org/field-guide/bird/golden-crowned-sparrow), [Audubon, Anna's Hummingbird](https://www.audubon.org/field-guide/bird/annas-hummingbird), [Audubon, House Finch](https://www.audubon.org/field-guide/bird/house-finch), [Audubon, Dark-eyed Junco](https://www.audubon.org/field-guide/bird/dark-eyed-junco), [Audubon, Black Phoebe](https://www.audubon.org/field-guide/bird/black-phoebe), [Audubon, California Towhee](https://www.audubon.org/field-guide/bird/california-towhee), [Audubon, Mourning Dove](https://www.audubon.org/field-guide/bird/mourning-dove), [Audubon, American Crow](https://www.audubon.org/field-guide/bird/american-crow), [Audubon, Common Raven](https://www.audubon.org/field-guide/bird/common-raven), [Audubon, Yellow-rumped Warbler](https://www.audubon.org/field-guide/bird/yellow-rumped-warbler), [Audubon, Great Horned Owl](https://www.audubon.org/field-guide/bird/great-horned-owl).

## Other animals around the Outer Sunset by season: salamanders, slugs, frogs, monarchs and turtles

Bay Nature calls rainy winter days the best time for amphibians, and says slender salamanders hide under planters, firewood and leaves in nearly every yard. iNaturalist records from Golden Gate Park to Lake Merced fit: salamander sightings are fewest in August and peak in March. Banana slugs are logged in every month, most in December. The Presidio says winter is the likeliest time to see one.

Pacific chorus frogs were nearly lost from San Francisco. Bay Nature lists Golden Gate Park's ponds, Pine Lake and Lake Merced among places that can hold them, and males call at ponds in the wet season. They are scarce here: seven iNaturalist records.

Monarch sightings peak in October and November. The Park Service names Rob Hill Campground in the Presidio as a wintering site, with 250 counted in 2016. No roost was found for the Sunset. Turtle sightings, mostly pond sliders, peak in April and May. November's most reported mammals are gray squirrels, raccoons and coyotes. For whales by month, see the ocean note.

## How a beginner starts birding near the Sunset: free walks, binoculars and easy places

The closest regular free walk is Golden Gate Bird Alliance's "Birding at the Garden" at the San Francisco Botanical Garden: first Sunday of the month, 8 to 11 a.m., meeting at the Main Gate. Beginners are welcome. Registration opens one week ahead, on Sunday at 8 a.m. The next first Sunday is December 6, 2026. The garden also hosts "Bird Curious" beginner walks. Golden Gate Bird Alliance's other field trips are mostly free but need sign-up, which opens the third Tuesday of each month at 1 p.m.

Binoculars help but are not required. Golden Gate Bird Alliance lends binoculars and scopes to members for up to a week, returned to its Berkeley office. A search of the San Francisco Public Library catalog on November 17, 2026 turned up books about binoculars, not binoculars to borrow.

For easy footing, Lake Merced's three birding stops (concrete bridge, Sunset Circle, Harding Park) have parking and are mostly paved, flat and wheelchair accessible. Sunset Dunes has fully accessible ramps at Judah and Taraval.
