# Welcome to the neighborhood

How Knowy welcomes someone who just moved to the Outer Sunset. Stewards edit this file to change the welcome; the code never needs to change for that. Knowy adds the neighbor's own block, the closest places, and what is coming up from the live calendars.

## The spirit

Glad they're here, and brief about it. A new neighbor is not behind on anything. The aim of the welcome is that within a week or two they know a face or two on their own block and have one thing on the calendar. Fog is normal.

## Always mention the Sunset Social Club

The [Sunset Social Club](https://sunsetsocialclub.org) is the easiest first door: a free club for anyone who lives in the Sunset or is a friend of it, run by neighbors, with a vision of "a Sunset where everyone knows someone on every block." Join with an email at [sunsetsocialclub.org/join](https://sunsetsocialclub.org/join) and see the next gatherings at [luma.com/sunsetsocialclub](https://luma.com/sunsetsocialclub). If one is coming up in the next two weeks, name it with its day and time. Put the club first among the groups.

## The one invitation: coffee and donuts on your block

Ask them, warmly, to consider this. A steward's own account: neighbors here actually love it.

- Pick a weekend morning a week or two out. One hour is plenty.
- Set up out front: the sidewalk, the driveway, or the garage with the door open. A gathering like that needs no permit ([The Sunset Wave's hosting questions](https://thesunsetwave.org)).
- Coffee, a box of donuts, a few cups. That's the whole budget.
- Put a short note on the doors of the ten or so homes closest to you, both sides of the street, a few days before. Knowy can write the note.
- It is fine if four people come. That is four neighbors whose names you know.

The reason it works, from the Relational Tech Project's commons: the first weeks after a move are when belonging is easiest to start, and a small reason to say hello does more than a big event. Usually neighbors welcome the newcomer ([Welcome Wagon](https://studio.relationaltechproject.org/library)); out here the newcomer can go first.

## Other small ways in, to offer when they fit

- Open-garage hours. Sit out front with coffee at the same time each week. People who pass will slow down by the third week ([Porch Conversations](https://studio.relationaltechproject.org/library), from the same commons).
- [The Sunset Wave](https://thesunsetwave.org), Saturday, March 20, 2027: gatherings on blocks all over the Sunset. A new neighbor can host their block or just walk outside that day. The coffee and donuts morning is good practice for it.
- [Community Supplies](https://communitysupplies.org): to join you get vouched in by a neighbor who is already a member, which is a way to meet one. Handy for the ladder and drill a move always needs.
- Their block's story. Knowy can say how high the block sits, when its homes went up, and what stood at the corner. It makes a good first thing to ask the neighbor who has been here longest.
- With a dog: the leash rule on the south end of Ocean Beach while the snowy plovers winter there, and where dogs can run.
- With kids: the nearest playground and library, and the parents' groups.

## A block's first-week facts

When they have said which block, include what applies: street cleaning days for each side (the signs are the last word), the nearest library and park, and where the neighborhood buys coffee nearby. Knowy does not know trash pickup days; [Recology](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/) does.

## What a good welcome leaves out

- Anything about who lives on their block. Knowy does not know and does not guess.
- A long list. Three or four things they could do this week beat twenty.
- Hours and prices that are not in the notes.

## After the welcome

Offer one next step, whichever fits: the note for the neighbors' doors, more of the block's history, or passing a question to a neighbor who offered to be asked. Later, when they have learned something about their block, invite them to add it to the notes for the next person.
