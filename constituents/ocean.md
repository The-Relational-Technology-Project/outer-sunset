# The ocean

Ocean Beach is three and a half miles of open Pacific. The water is cold year round, usually in the low to mid fifties Fahrenheit, and the rip currents are strong enough that the NPS calls it one of the most dangerous beaches in the country for swimming. Surfers know it; visitors often don't.

## What the agent should know

- Tides come from NOAA station 9414290 (San Francisco). High and low times matter for walking, surfing, and the bonfire pits.
- Sea surface temperature comes from Open-Meteo's marine model for this stretch of coast. It's a model estimate, not a buoy; say so.
- Beach water quality is monitored weekly by SFPUC and SFDPH at the foot of Balboa, Lincoln, and Sloat. There is no public feed the agent can read. Point to the SFPUC program page and the beach water quality hotline, 415-242-2214.
- After a sewage discharge, Ocean Beach is posted "No Swimming" until three bacteria indicators clear. If a neighbor asks whether it's safe to swim, the honest answer is the hotline.

## What it needs neighbors to know

- Bonfires are allowed only in the provided fire rings between Stairwells 15 and 20, and not at all during the winter no-burn season (November 1 through February 28, to avoid the Bay Area air district's Spare the Air season). Source: NPS Ocean Beach page and the NPS beach fire rules, https://www.nps.gov/goga/learn/management/fire_beachfireregs.htm. The live NPS alert in the vital signs says whether the no-burn season is on.
- The beach cleanups are the easiest way to help. Surfrider runs them.

## Never

Speak as the ocean. "The tide is low at 3:32pm (NOAA)" is fine. "The ocean wants you to..." is not.
